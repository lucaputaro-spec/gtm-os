Sort the answer that comes back

Every outbound email gets one of a handful of replies. Each needs a different move. Only the newly written part counts: quoted history and email footers are set aside first. Fixed rules catch opt-outs and out-of-office notes, unclear cases go to a person, and a language model sorts the rest and drafts the answer.

One pipeline for all of it

Each step above writes to the same record. That is the difference between three scripts and a system: a salesperson opens one card and sees the whole story. Click a card to pick it for the next reply. Nothing here is sent or synced to a CRM: the board shows what would happen and lives only in your browser.

    How I build these for clients

    Rules decide what must not go wrong.
    Scores, owners and detected opt-outs come from fixed rules you can read and change. The language model sorts the remaining replies into one of seven categories and drafts the text. A wrong sentence is cheap, a missed opt-out is not.
    Every score shows its reasons.
    A salesperson who cannot see why a lead ranks first will stop trusting the ranking. Each point here comes with the line that earned it.
    It keeps working when the model does not.
    If the model is down or the daily budget is used up, a template takes over and the page says so. No lead waits because an API had a bad minute.
    Speed is the product.
    A Harvard Business Review study on online leads concluded that “most companies are not responding nearly fast enough.” Read the study. The reply above was ready in seconds.
